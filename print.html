Shoot Wednesday, edit Thursday

  1. Set up — tripod at eye level, window on your face, four props laid out left to right. 15 min
  2. Per video: read the hook, what it is and over to you off the prompter. Look away for so what (your story) and just say it. Ten seconds of the prop alone. 15 min each
  3. Two takes maximum. Stumbles stay in. Do not edit on the shoot day — straight after, you can only see yourself, not the video.
  4. Thursday: top and tail, cut dead air over one second, two text cards, cutaway to the object over the boring bit. 15 min each, timer on

The claim test

Could a sensible person argue with this out loud?

If nobody could, it is not a claim. It is a fact or a feeling, and the video will have no spine. Put it back in the vault.

The five parts of a video

  1. The claim. What you're arguing. Written first, never said out loud. It decides everything else.
  2. The hook. First three seconds. A strange thing to look at, and a line that doesn't follow from it.
  3. What it is. Teach something real and checkable. 20–30 seconds.
  4. So what. Your story, and the bit underneath you'd rather not say. Your words only — nobody writes this one for you.
  5. Over to you. One small thing tonight, the dispute line, then the sign-off. ok bye.

One object you can hold, on camera, every single time.

One video a week is a complete week.

There are no streaks. Nothing accumulates. You do not owe next week anything.

The floor: one Strange Things I Learnt. No props, no set, nobody else. You and a table, ninety seconds.

What you shoot Wednesday goes out next week. You are always a week ahead, so nothing is ever due today.

After a bad week

Make one video before you are allowed to improve the system. Not the book, not the app, not the vault, not the filing.

Rebuilding the system is the most sophisticated form of avoidance available to you, because it feels like work.
